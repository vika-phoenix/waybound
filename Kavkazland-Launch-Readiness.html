P3

Cancellation and cooling-off, stated in full

verified in code

Three rules stack, in this order. The first that applies wins.

1 · cooling-off window — platform rule, a guide cannot override it

SchemeWindowHow the money is heldStatus
flat 30 min
every booking		 Charged at booking, like any shop. Cancel inside the window and the whole amount comes back, card fee included. live
tiered 24 h if >30 days out
2 h at 8–30 days
30 min at 7 days or less		 Card authorised at booking, charged when the window shuts. Cancelling inside it drops the authorisation — no charge, and no refund to wait for. standby

Switching is one environment variable, COOLING_OFF_SCHEME, followed by python tools/cooling_sync.py --write, which rewrites the wording on 16 pages across both languages so no page can promise terms the code does not keep.

why flat is the live one — the merchant-fee question

  • A refund never returns the processor's fee. Under flat, every cancellation inside the window costs you roughly 2.8–3.5% of that booking, absorbed by you. Thirty minutes is short enough that few travellers use it, so the loss is small and the machinery is simple: no authorisations outstanding, no capture to schedule, nothing to fail a day later.
  • Under tiered, a 24-hour window would make charge-then-refund expensive, so it holds the card instead and charges only once the window closes. A cancellation inside the window then costs nobody anything.
  • But tiered only helps on instruments that can be held. SBP is a bank transfer and cannot be authorised at all, and YooKassa's wallet methods hold for two hours, which is under the longest window. Both keep being charged outright whichever scheme is running.
  • You are launching YooKassa-first with SBP prominent, so tiered's saving would apply to card payments only. Stay on flat for launch. Revisit if card volume grows and in-window cancellations turn out to cost real money.

2 · repeat-use limit

Two free-window cancellations per account in a rolling 30 days. The third booking is taken normally but granted no window, so the tour's cancellation policy applies from the start. Nothing is ever blocked. The count includes only cancellations the traveller made themselves inside a window — a guide cancelling, a departure being called off, or a refund you issued never count against them.

3 · the tour's cancellation policy, once the window closes

A guide may set their own, stricter or more generous, shown before payment and snapshotted at booking so later edits cannot reach an existing traveller. Where a guide sets none, this is the platform default:

Days before departureRefundKept back
30+Full, less a 1% processing fee1%
14–29Half50%
0–13None100%

the guide's side

  • A guide can cancel a confirmed booking at any point up to departure. The traveller receives 100% of everything paid — no policy tiers, no timing test — and the guide is paid nothing for it.
  • The guide must now give a reason. It reaches the traveller's email and inbox, and reaches you in the alert.
  • Your alert carries that guide's running total, and the admin user list has a Cancelled column that turns amber at one and red at three. There is no automatic penalty — that call is yours.
  • Guides are told in writing, in both languages, that repeated cancellations may pause their listings or remove their account.

Confirmed as current. Window lengths, the repeat-use limit, the guide-cancels-full-refund rule and the default tiers are all pinned by tests, and a build-time check proves all 16 published pages state the live scheme in both languages. The one judgement left to you is whether 1% at 30 days or more is the right number — a pricing decision, not a correctness one.

Charging in euros

decision aid

Not a task — a reference for when European travellers start mattering. Two halves that are usually confused: presenting a price in euros is easy, settling in euros is not.

EUR 1

What your account can and cannot do

Your Stripe account is registered as a US corporation, and that decides everything below. Entity type is irrelevant here — a US sole trader settles in dollars exactly as a US company does.

Possible todayWhy
Show and charge € on StripeYesPresentment currency is independent of account country. The customer sees euros and their bank does no conversion
Receive € from Stripe without conversionNoA US account settles in USD. Stripe converts and charges you for it
Hold a € balance on PayPalYesPayPal lets a US business hold multiple currency balances — the one route to euros without an EU entity
Settle € to a Wise or Payoneer IBANNoStripe requires the payout account to match the account country. A foreign IBAN does not get around it

Genuine EUR settlement on Stripe means a Stripe account registered in a SEPA country, which means a legal entity there. That is the requirement there is no way around.

EUR 2

The drawbacks, in the order they will bite

read before deciding
  • The fee moves to you, it does not disappear. Today a European customer’s own bank converts and charges them 1–3%. Price in euros and that stops — then Stripe converts EUR to USD on the way to your account and bills you roughly 1–2% instead. You are buying the customer’s goodwill with your margin. That can be worth it, because showing local currency lifts checkout completion, often by more than 2% — but it is a bet to measure, not a saving.
  • PayPal’s spread is worse than Stripe’s. If you hold euros on PayPal and later convert them, expect around 3–4%, against Stripe’s 1–2%. Holding a EUR balance only pays off if you actually spend euros. Otherwise you have moved the conversion to the more expensive provider and delayed it.
  • Refunds carry exchange-rate risk, and your cooling-off promise makes that concrete. You charge at today’s rate and refund at the rate on the day of the refund. Your published guarantee is that a cancellation inside the window returns everything, card fee included — but if the rate moved, the customer sees a different number land in their account than the one that left it. Small in absolute terms, and exactly the kind of thing that generates a support message accusing you of shortchanging them.
  • Two currencies to reconcile. Tour priced in one, charged in another, settled in a third. Deposit and balance can be taken weeks apart at different rates, so a booking no longer has one clean number attached to it.
  • Displayed price can drift from charged price. Rates are cached for 24 hours. A euro figure shown on a listing and the euro figure at checkout can disagree if the rate moves inside that window, which is a complaint at best and a consumer-law problem at worst.
EUR 3

What would actually have to be switched on

stripe

  • Activate the live account first. None of this is reachable in test mode.
  • Nothing to enable for EUR itself — card charges accept a currency per transaction. It is a code change, not a dashboard toggle.
  • Enable European payment methods, under Settings → Payment methods. SEPA Direct Debit, iDEAL in the Netherlands, Bancontact in Belgium, giropay in Germany. In several European markets these convert far better than cards, and most of them require EUR presentment to appear at all — which is often the real reason to price in euros.
  • Strong Customer Authentication is mandatory on European cards under PSD2. Stripe Checkout handles the 3-D Secure step for you, so nothing to build — but expect a visibly longer checkout, and a higher abandon rate than you see on Russian cards.
  • SCA interacts badly with the tiered cooling-off scheme. An authorisation that is held and captured later can require re-authentication if it sits too long, and iDEAL and SEPA cannot be authorised-then-captured at all. If you ever run tiered alongside European methods, that combination needs testing before it is trusted.

paypal

  • Live app credentials, and PAYPAL_ENV=live.
  • Add EUR to your account under Money → Currencies. Without it PayPal auto-converts every euro payment to USD on arrival, at its own spread, and the decision is made for you.
  • Set the auto-convert preference deliberately — hold euros, or convert on receipt. Leaving the default is choosing the expensive option by not choosing.

in the code

  • USD is hardcoded in three places in backend/apps/payments/international.py, and a tour carries a single currency.
  • What is missing is a presentment layer: choose the display currency from the visitor, convert once, and keep the deposit and balance split in one consistent currency so balance_due stays correct.
  • The FX source is already shared — both rails use the same cached CBR rates, so there is one place to change and nothing to re-price by hand.

Recommendation: not yet. You have no European bookings to measure, the work is real, and the saving is negative until conversion lift pays for the fee you would be absorbing. Revisit when European traffic is worth counting. Until then the currency problem that costs you money is the other one — a Russian traveller cannot pay at all until YooKassa is live.
